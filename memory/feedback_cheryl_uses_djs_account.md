Learned the hard way 2026-09-28. A whole card session I ran assuming I was talking to **DJ** was actually **Cheryl** — **she has access to DJ's account** (same login, same session identity, windowandsolarcare@gmail.com).

**What I got wrong because of the bad assumption:** I spoke to a NON-technical person in full jargon the entire time — pixel dimensions, aspect ratios, "inpaint/pan," "scrim," "safe margin," Midjourney flags, "separate browser session / login." I also pushed technical tasks on her (log into a controlled browser tab, run the MJ editor). That is the exact opposite of the #1 Cheryl rule (plain language, never make her learn a tool — see [[feedback_cheryl_card_workflow]]).

**Rule:**
- **The account/session does NOT tell you who the human is.** Cheryl operates on DJ's account. Never assume the person is DJ just because it's his account.
- **DEFAULT to plain, warm, non-technical language for any design/card collaboration** (and whenever unsure who you're talking to). Plain language never hurts DJ (a printer); jargon badly hurts Cheryl. So plain is the safe default.
- **When it's ambiguous who you're talking to, ASK early** — a light "is this DJ or Cheryl?" — and calibrate tone/depth accordingly. Signals it may be Cheryl: it's a card/marketing/design ask, real-estate content, or the conversation fits the Cheryl card loop.
- If it IS Cheryl: run the [[feedback_cheryl_card_workflow]] loop — collaborate on copy (don't author it), one stable link, present a proof, she runs Midjourney and you take the downloaded file.

**Why:** getting a technically-correct piece to the right spec is still a FAIL if it was delivered by talking over the user's head. Tone/altitude is part of the deliverable for Cheryl.

**How to apply:** at the start of a design conversation, if there's any chance it's Cheryl, use plain language from the first sentence and confirm identity if unsure. Don't wait to be corrected.
