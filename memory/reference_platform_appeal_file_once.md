**The lesson, proven when Window & Solar Care's Google Business Profile appeal WON on the first clean filing (submitted 2026-09-27, reinstated 2026-09-28, case `9-2903000041323`).**

**Rule: file a platform appeal/reinstatement ONCE, CLEAN.** For any account-level appeal to a big regulated platform (Google Business Profile, Meta/Facebook, Apple, Stripe/payment processors, app stores), a repeat or sloppy attempt can get the WHOLE account actioned — not just the one listing. So you do NOT iterate ("try something, see what sticks"). You get everything airtight FIRST, then submit exactly one well-built appeal.

**The three things that made it airtight (copy this shape):**
1. **Name the EXACT reason Google gave, from the primary source** — DJ read the original suspension email verbatim ("flagged for suspicious activity"). We had earlier *inferred* "deceptive content" and that was WRONG; appealing against a reason the platform never gave wastes the one shot. Always quote the platform's own words, not a guess.
2. **ONE provable address, consistent across listing ⇄ registration ⇄ evidence.** The linchpin was a single real address (DJ's home, `32569 San Miguelito Dr, Thousand Palms CA 92276`) that matched the CA LLC Articles AND the federal EIN letter. Those two government docs stood in for the utility bill Google asked for (home-based, 2-month-old LLC, utilities in the owner's personal name) — and we EXPLAINED that substitution in the appeal notes rather than hoping Google wouldn't notice.
3. **Pre-clean every surface the platform can see BEFORE filing** — NAP (name/address/phone) made consistent on the website (phone → 760-334-5355 everywhere), app code, and external directories (Yelp, MapQuest dupes, etc.), because Google reads the whole search index, not just the one profile.

**★ Reinstated ≠ fully manageable.** GBP sent TWO emails the same day: one confirming reinstatement ("no further action to verify at this time" — that was about the APPEAL), and a SEPARATE "Further verification is required to manage your account" / "your edits can be visible to customers" email. These are different Google steps — winning the appeal does not complete the ownership/manage verification. After any reinstatement, check whether a distinct Get-verified step is still pending before assuming the owner can edit the live listing.

**Why:** DJ kept hand-catching the same NAP-drift class for months; the durable fix is a surfaces-this-touches decision log (proposed: a "what does this touch" + checked_on field surfaced monthly) so a change like a new phone number can't silently go un-propagated and re-trigger a flag.

**How to apply:** before filing ANY platform appeal — (a) get the exact reason in the platform's words; (b) make the identity evidence internally consistent and explain any substitution; (c) scrub every public surface; (d) submit once; (e) after a win, look for a separate verification step.
