DJ (2026-09-05): "The Robert and Linnea lesson is NOT the technical stuff — it's the **caressing of words, dates, and actions.** Learn my instincts and record them for future Operator sessions." This is the feel of how DJ runs his business. Match it.

## WORDS (tone + wording)
- **Warm and personal, never salesy or high-pressure.** DJ cut my second Wednesday push ("Wednesday's the one I'd love to lock in" AFTER the link): *"one good push before the link, don't circle back and push again — too much pressure."* → **Make the case ONCE, then stop.** Trust it.
- **Lead with the human, not the transaction.** Linnea had called 3× and felt ignored → open with *"So sorry I missed your calls — I've got you all taken care of now,"* and acknowledge her life (*"so glad things are back to normal after the flood"*). Genuine care first, logistics second.
- **Use the relationship.** Referral → *"any friend of Jim Leal's is a friend of mine."* Long-time customer → reference the history. It's a small-business, know-your-customer voice.
- **Sound like DJ:** friendly, first-name, casual, concise; signs **"- Dan"**; plain spoken (he has replies read aloud). No corporate stiffness, no emoji-spam, no walls of text.

## DATES (timing + scheduling instincts)
- **Batch by geography — always.** Cluster same-neighborhood jobs on the same day (the PGA West Wednesday run: Jim + Rik + Robert; the Hemet Wednesday: Blair + Barbra + Linnea). Route efficiency is a reflex. **Pitch the day that batches best for DJ — but frame it as the best day for the customer.**
- **Honor the customer's real-life constraints — read their words for them.** "Not Tuesday, my cleaning lady comes Tuesday." "The week after." "Anytime after 9." "I can't wait around till 1pm." Catch these in their thread/voicemails and schedule AROUND them. Booking before you know their window = a redo.
- **Offer a TIGHT set, not scattershot.** DJ dropped a 3rd slot: *"can't wait around till that time, just offer 2."* Give 2–3 good options, weighted toward the day you want.
- **Earliest slot that fits their window.**

## ACTIONS (sequencing + judgment)
- **Two quote types — know which you're in:** (1) quote-and-GET-it on the spot → push the price onto the current job now; (2) quote-and-WAIT → separate quote, decide later. (See the quote-push build.)
- **Review-then-send, ALWAYS.** Never auto-send a customer message. Draft it, put it in DJ's HUD prefilled, HE reads it and hits send. Same for confirmations/offers.
- **Minimize back-and-forth for everyone** — prefer tap-to-book links so the customer "just presses buttons," no texting tag.
- **New customer → price stays TBD** until DJ sees the home. Don't guess a price.
- **A personal call beats a text when someone's been reaching for DJ personally** (Linnea's repeated calls warranted a warm personal touch, not a cold batch confirm).
- **Gather constraints + ask ONE clarifying question, then do the WHOLE thing in one pass** — don't book, then re-book (that's what made Robert/Linnea drag).

## THE OVERARCHING INSTINCT
Every customer touch should feel like it came from a caring small-business owner who knows them, respects their time, and isn't pushy — delivered efficiently (batched, tap-to-book, one pass), and always passed under DJ's eye before it goes out.
