DJ's hiring philosophy, clarified in his own words 2026-10-02 (correcting the loose "7/10 who won't bolt" framing):

He is **NOT** mainly worried a top candidate will quit/flake. His real thesis: a true **10/10 is expensive** (commands the highest pay rate), **gets grabbed fast** by bigger employers, and you'd be stretching to both win and keep him — perpetually at risk of a better offer. **He does not want to be the shop overpaying for the single best guy and scrambling because that person is so valuable/in-demand.**

What he wants = the **RIGHT-FIT** hire: someone genuinely glad to have *this* job at *his* rate ($23–27/hr route tech), solid and dependable, who settles in and STAYS because it's a good match for them — not because he out-bid the market. Value sweet spot, not top of market.

**Why:** the last (Indeed) hire failed on fit/retention, not raw talent. Over-indexing on the flashiest/most-credentialed résumé pulls in "overqualified" people who leave the moment something better appears — the exact churn DJ is trying to avoid, and at a premium wage he doesn't need to pay.

**How to apply (HR ranking lens):** do NOT auto-rank by most-impressive résumé. Down-weight candidates who look over-qualified for a $23–27 route job (flight risk). Up-weight: genuine interest in THIS kind of work, stability/tenure signals, local + reliable, realistic wage expectations, someone for whom this is a good step (not a step down they'll abandon). Screen interviews/questions should probe "is this the job you actually want and will stay in?" over "are you the most talented applicant?"
